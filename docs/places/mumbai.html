Mumbai

Map of Bombay, 1893 AD. At the time when Prabhu was there.
Mumbai
Region Maharashtra, India
Also known as Bombay (historical)
Significance Shatavdhan venue; business headquarters; meeting place with Gandhiji
Period V.S. 1942–1957 (1886–1901 AD)
Key locations Framjee Cawasjee Institute; Mani Bhavan; Kalbadevi; Santacruz; Girgaon
Key events Shatavdhan (V.S. 1943); Jewellery business est. (V.S. 1946); Met Gandhiji (V.S. 1947)

Mumbai — then known as Bombay — was the centre of Param Krupalu Dev's professional life and the stage for several landmark events. He first visited the city in V.S. 1942 (1886 AD) for avdhan demonstrations, performed the historic Shatavdhan at Framjee Cawasjee Institute in V.S. 1943 (1887), and later established the jewellery firm Revashankar Jagjivan Company in V.S. 1946 (1890). It was in Mumbai that He first met Mahatma Gandhi in V.S. 1947 (1891). Param Krupalu Dev resided here, conducting business and spiritual discourses, until He began withdrawing from worldly affairs from V.S. 1952 onwards. The city in the 1890s looked vastly different from today — Juhu and much of the western coast were ocean, Santacruz (where He first stayed) was a small island of land, and the two main railway lines ran from Bori Bandar (now Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus) northward.

› Avdhans in Mumbai

Main article: Avdhans

In V.S. 1942, Bhadarva (1886 AD), Param Krupalu Dev first travelled to Mumbai for avdhan demonstrations. He also had financial prospects in mind, as the family was in severe financial difficulty — His mother Devba's ornaments had been mortgaged and two sisters were still unmarried. En route, He stopped at Jetpar at the home of brother-in-law Shri Chatrabhuj Bechar, where astrologer Shri Shankar Pancholi prepared His horoscope and predicted great fame and wealth within one year.

In Mumbai, Param Krupalu Dev performed avdhans at the Theosophical Society in Girgaum, demonstrating His extraordinary powers in nine languages. He also performed avdhans extensively across the city in V.S. 1943 (1887 AD), culminating in the historic Shatavdhan at Framjee Cawasjee Institute. The astrologer's prediction proved half-true — He attained great fame, but not wealth — which led Param Krupalu Dev to investigate astrology further, eventually mastering palmistry, horoscopy, and face-reading to such a degree that He could tell a person's birth time and past and future simply by looking at them.

› Shatavdhan at Framjee Cawasjee Institute

Main article: Avdhans

On January 22, 1887 (Saturday, Maha Vad 13, V.S. 1943), Param Krupalu Dev performed the historic Shatavdhan — simultaneously attending to 100 tasks — at Framjee Cawasjee Institute in Mumbai. The event was presided over by Dr. Peterson and attended by Sir Charles Sergeant and other dignitaries. Param Krupalu Dev was awarded a gold medal and honoured with the title 'Sakshat Saraswati' (Goddess of Knowledge incarnate).

Among the attendees was Shri Maneklal Ghelabhai of Vadodara, whose father was a renowned jeweller. Impressed by Param Krupalu Dev's demonstration, Maneklal sought a conversation with Him. When Param Krupalu Dev mentioned that He needed to earn a livelihood but did not know how, Maneklal agreed to teach Him the art of jewellery valuation. He would later become one of the partners when Param Krupalu Dev established His jewellery firm. British and Gujarati newspapers — including Mumbai Samachar, Lokmitra, and Nyayadarshak — published articles praising His extraordinary feat.

This event proved to be a turning point. While it brought tremendous fame, it also awakened deep vairagya (detachment) in Param Krupalu Dev. The very next day, He resolved to never again accept invitations for avdhan demonstrations, as the public adulation was not the goal of His life — He sought only to be a devoted seeker of Truth.

› Arrival for business

As the eldest son of the family, Param Krupalu Dev could not pursue diksha (renunciation) — the family's financial situation was dire, His mother Devba's ornaments had been mortgaged, and two sisters were yet unmarried. Just before Paryushan of V.S. 1942, He resolved to earn a livelihood and came to Bombay. When He first arrived in the city for business, He stayed for six months in the basement of the home of Shri Tribhuvan Bhanji in Santacruz.

Using His mastery of astrology, palmistry and face-reading, Param Krupalu Dev advised Shri Revashankarbhai Jagjivan that a legal career would not suit him, and that coming to Mumbai for the jewellery business would be profitable. Revashankarbhai heeded this advice, left Morbi, and eventually partnered with Param Krupalu Dev to establish the Revashankar Jagjivan Company.

During His early days in Mumbai, Param Krupalu Dev narrated the story of His Jatismaranjnan (recollection of past lives) to Shri Padamshi Thakarshi at a Digambar Jain temple in the city. This account became one of the important records of His extraordinary spiritual experiences.

› Revashankar Jagjivan Company

Mani Bhavan — residence of the Jagjivan brothers where Gandhiji first met Param Krupalu Dev

In Ashadh V.S. 1945 (1889 AD), Shri Revashankarbhai Jagjivan moved from Morbi to Mumbai. By Bhadarva of the same year, Param Krupalu Dev joined him as a partner in the jewellery business. On Fagan Vad 11, V.S. 1946 (1890 AD), the firm was formally established as Revashankar Jagjivan Company, with three partners: Param Krupalu Dev, Revashankarbhai, and Shri Maneklal Ghelabhai (who brought his expertise in jewellery valuation).

The firm was named after Revashankarbhai as he was the primary financier. The residence of the Jagjivan brothers was Mani Bhavan, which would later become famous as the place where Gandhiji first met Param Krupalu Dev. Revashankarbhai valued Param Krupalu Dev's ethical perfection and knew He could manage the company with integrity. In V.S. 1948 (1892 AD), Shri Naginchand Kapurchand from Surat and Shri Chhotalal Lallubhai from Ahmedabad joined the firm as additional partners.

The business required frequent travel to Surat, Bharuch, Ahmedabad, and other Gujarat towns. Initially, Param Krupalu Dev was staying in Santacruz at Shri Tribhuvan Bhanji's home, but the commute to the business premises in the city was difficult, so He shifted to a residence in Girgaon. At its peak, the business obligated Param Krupalu Dev so heavily that from V.S. 1948 to 1951, He entered a period of virtual silence — curtailing spiritual writing and discussions.

From V.S. 1952 (1896 AD), Param Krupalu Dev began withdrawing from business, expressing His desire to retire. His younger brother Shri Mansukhbhai joined the firm to take over responsibilities. Param Krupalu Dev stayed on briefly as an advisor. By V.S. 1955 (1899 AD), He completely retired from the business, willing His entire share of the assets to Mansukhbhai.

› Meeting with Gandhiji

In Jeth V.S. 1947 (1891 AD), Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi — who had just returned from England as a newly qualified barrister — first met Param Krupalu Dev at the home of Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta in Mumbai. Dr. Pranjivandas, who was Param Krupalu Dev's wife Jhabakbai's brother, introduced the two. This meeting was the beginning of a profound spiritual relationship that would last the rest of Param Krupalu Dev's life.

Gandhiji was deeply impressed and would later describe Param Krupalu Dev as the one who most influenced his spiritual development. Even after leaving for South Africa in V.S. 1949 (1893 AD), Gandhiji continued to correspond with Param Krupalu Dev, seeking His guidance on questions of religion, ethics, and self-knowledge. When he received the news of Param Krupalu Dev's passing in 1901, Gandhiji wrote from Durban to Revashankarbhai expressing his profound grief.

› Later years in Mumbai

In V.S. 1949 (1893 AD), Pujyashri Lalluji Muni spent the four monsoon months at an upashray (monks' dwelling) in Chinchpokli, Mumbai, and visited Param Krupalu Dev daily during this period. These regular meetings deepened the spiritual bond between the two.

After His extensive spiritual retreats in the Charotar region and Idar (V.S. 1952–1955), Param Krupalu Dev returned briefly to Mumbai in V.S. 1955 (1899 AD). His health had begun to decline, and He was later taken to Matunga, Sion, and Tithal (near Valsad) for recovery. By Magsar Vad of V.S. 1957 (~1900–1901), He arrived in Mumbai from Ahmedabad with His health severely deteriorating, before departing for Wadhwan and ultimately Rajkot, where He left His mortal body.
