Wadhwan Camp (present: Surendranagar)

Wadhwan Camp city skyline
Wadhwan Camp
Region Surendranagar district, Gujarat
Significance 16 avdhans performed; famous photographs taken; Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal founded; final stay before Rajkot
Period V.S. ~1940 & 1956–1957 (1884 & 1900–1901 AD)
Key locations Limbdi State guest house; photo studio
Key events 16 avdhans; Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal founded (V.S. 1956); Pragnavbodh Mokshamala dictated; photographs taken

Wadhwan Camp (present-day Surendranagar), in Gujarat, holds deep significance in Param Krupalu Dev's life both as an early site of His avdhan demonstrations and as the place where He spent some of His final months. He performed 16 avdhans here before 2,000 people in His youth, and later, in V.S. 1956–1957, He stayed at the Limbdi State guest house, founded the Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal, dictated the 108 lessons of Pragnavbodh Mokshamala, and had His famous photographs taken — all while His body was in a state of extreme austerity and dispassion.

› Avdhans in Wadhwan

Main article: Avdhans

In approximately V.S. 1940 (~1884 AD), Param Krupalu Dev performed 16 avdhans in Wadhwan before an audience of 2,000 people, including Colonel H. L. Natsaheb. This remarkable demonstration was covered by Gujarati and English newspapers including Mumbai Samachar, Lokmitra, and Nyayadarshak, further spreading His fame across Gujarat and beyond.

› Final stay in Wadhwan Camp

On Shravan Vad 10, V.S. 1956 (1900 AD), Param Krupalu Dev left Morbi for Wadhwan Camp, where He stayed at the Limbdi State guest house. His health had already been declining due to colitis, but His spiritual radiance was at its peak. During this stay, He dictated the 108 lessons of Pragnavbodh Mokshamala — a systematic work for spiritual seekers.

› Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal

During His stay in Wadhwan Camp in V.S. 1956 (1900 AD), Param Krupalu Dev founded the Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal — an organisation dedicated to the propagation of the highest spiritual truths (Paramshrut). This body would later play an important role in preserving and publishing Param Krupalu Dev's writings and teachings, including the second edition of the collected works 'Shrimad Rajchandra' (V.S. 1982 / ~1926 AD), which carried a preface by Mahatma Gandhi.

› Famous photographs

Main article: Chitrapats

At the request of Shri Sukhlalbhai, two photographs of Param Krupalu Dev were taken during His Wadhwan stay — one in a lotus posture (padmasana) and one in a standing meditative pose. These photographs, capturing His state of extreme dispassion and austerity, became among the most revered images in the Shrimad Rajchandra tradition.

Param Krupalu Dev later told His brother Mansukhbhai that these photographs had captured His inner state in the way that the image of Lord Parshwanath during Kamath's torment captures the Lord's equanimity — suggesting that His physical suffering from illness was analogous to the tests faced by great souls.

› Departure for Rajkot

On Kartak Vad 7, V.S. 1957 (~1900), Param Krupalu Dev left Wadhwan Camp for Ahmedabad, where He stayed 27 days before travelling to Mumbai for medical treatment. After returning to Wadhwan Camp on Maha Vad 6, V.S. 1957 (~1901), His body had deteriorated dramatically — reduced from 130–140 pounds to merely 57 pounds.

On Fagan Sud 6, V.S. 1957, Param Krupalu Dev left Wadhwan Camp for the last time and proceeded to Rajkot, where He would spend His final days and leave His mortal body on Chaitra Vad 5, V.S. 1957 (April 9, 1901).
