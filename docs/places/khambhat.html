Khambhat

Khambhat (Cambay), Gujarat
Khambhat (Cambay)
Region Gujarat, India
Significance Native place of Ambalalbhai; Lalluji Muni's initiation; first meeting of Param Krupalu Dev and Lalluji Muni
Period V.S. 1940–1963 (1884–1907 AD)
Key locations Sthanakvasi Upashray (monks' dwelling); Jain shrines
Key events Lalluji Muni initiated (V.S. 1940); first meeting of Param Krupalu Dev & Lalluji Muni on Dhanteras V.S. 1946; Ambalalbhai's passing (V.S. 1963)

Khambhat (historically known as Cambay) is an ancient port city in Gujarat that occupies a pivotal place in the spiritual lineage of Shrimad Rajchandra. It was the native place of Shri Ambalalbhai Lalchand, one of Param Krupalu Dev's foremost disciples. In Khambhat, Shri Lalluji Muni was initiated as a Sthanakvasi monk in V.S. 1940 (1884 AD), and it was here — on Dhanteras, V.S. 1946 (1890 AD) — that the historic first meeting between Param Krupalu Dev and Lalluji Muni took place at the Sthanakvasi Upashray. The city also witnessed the passing of Ambalalbhai on Chaitra Vad 12, V.S. 1963 (1907 AD), after a life of extraordinary devotion.

› Lalluji Muni's initiation

In V.S. 1940 (1884 AD), Lalluji — who would later become one of the most important spiritual figures in the Shrimad Rajchandra tradition — was initiated as a Sthanakvasi monk in Khambhat. The Sthanakvasi sect, a reformist Jain tradition, had a significant presence in the city, and the Upashray (monks' dwelling) in Khambhat served as the site of this initiation.

Following his initiation, Lalluji Muni studied Jain scriptures and practiced austerities. However, it was not until his encounter with Shrimad Rajchandra six years later that his spiritual trajectory would be decisively transformed.

› First meeting with Lalluji Muni

On Aso Vad 12, V.S. 1946 (Dhanteras, 1890 AD), Shrimad Rajchandra visited Khambhat at the request of Shri Ambalalbhai. This visit led to the momentous first meeting between Param Krupalu Dev and Shri Lalluji Muni at the Sthanakvasi Upashray.

This meeting proved to be a turning point for Lalluji Muni, who recognised in the young Param Krupalu Dev a spiritual depth and authority that he had not encountered among the monks and scholars of his own tradition. From this point onward, Lalluji Muni became a devoted disciple of Param Krupalu Dev, ultimately serving as the primary instrument for preserving and propagating His teachings after His passing.

Shri Tribhuvanbhai was also connected with Param Krupalu Dev through Khambhat.

› Retreats near Khambhat

In V.S. 1947 (1891 AD), Param Krupalu Dev spent Paryushan in the village of Ralaj, near Khambhat, where He composed four poems. This was shortly after His attainment of Shuddh Samyagdarshan (self-realisation).

During His retreat period in V.S. 1952 (1896 AD), Param Krupalu Dev again visited the Khambhat area. He stayed at the home of Shri Chhotalal Manekchand in Khambhat, as part of His extensive spiritual retreats across the Charotar region of Gujarat. These visits reinforced Khambhat's place as a recurring touchstone in Param Krupalu Dev's itinerary during His years of intensive spiritual practice.

› Ambalalbhai's legacy

Shri Ambalalbhai Lalchand of Khambhat was among the most important of Param Krupalu Dev's disciples. In V.S. 1945 (1889 AD), young Ambalalbhai and Shri Chhotalalbhai went to Ahmedabad for a wedding, where they met Shri Juthabhai — an encounter that first drew Ambalalbhai towards Param Krupalu Dev's orbit.

In the final period of Param Krupalu Dev's life, during a 27-day stay in Ahmedabad (V.S. 1957), Param Krupalu Dev showered "unprecedented wisdom" upon Ambalalbhai, leading to his attainment of self-realisation. After Param Krupalu Dev's passing, Ambalalbhai collaborated with Mansukhbhai to publish the first edition of Shrimad Rajchandra (collected works) in V.S. 1961 (1905 AD).

On Chaitra Vad 12, V.S. 1963 (1907 AD), Shri Ambalalbhai passed away in Khambhat at the age of 37, with the words 'Sahajatmaswaroop Hey Prabhu' (O Lord, the innate form of the Self) on his lips — a testament to the depth of his realisation.
