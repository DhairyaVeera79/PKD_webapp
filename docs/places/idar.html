Idar

Idar city skyline
Idar
Region North Gujarat, India
Significance Past-life connection; site of final spiritual retreat; Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta's residence
Period V.S. 1953 & 1955–1956 (1897 & 1899–1900 AD)
Key locations Dr. Pranjivandas's residence; Caves in the hills; Chandan caves; Pudhvishila; Siddhashila
Key events 6-week cave retreat (V.S. 1955); proclamation of renunciation; taught Bruhad Dravyasangrah to monks

Idar, in north Gujarat, holds a singular place in Param Krupalu Dev's life — both as the site of a past-life connection and as the location of His most intensive spiritual retreat. In one of His recalled previous births, Param Krupalu Dev had been a Digambar Sadhu in Idar. In this life, He visited Idar twice: first in V.S. 1953 (1897 AD) with Saubhagyabhai, and later in V.S. 1955 (1899 AD) for a profound six-week retreat at the residence of Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta, who served as Chief Medical Officer of Idar State. During this retreat, Param Krupalu Dev meditated in the hills and ancient caves, taught scriptures to monks, and formally proclaimed His renunciation of all worldly ties.

› Past life connection

Through His Jatismaranjnan (recollection of past lives), Param Krupalu Dev recalled that in a birth prior to His incarnation as a Rajkumar (prince) in Nepal/Tibet, He had been a Digambar Sadhu (unclad ascetic monk) in Idar. This monk was an extraordinary scholar of Jainism. He was poisoned by someone envious of His knowledge. Upon realising He had been poisoned, the Sadhu entered kayotsarg (meditative stillness) and left His body in Idar in that state of equanimity. This karmic connection with Idar would bring Param Krupalu Dev back to the place in His final life.

› Visit with Saubhagyabhai

In Vaishakh V.S. 1953 (1897 AD), Param Krupalu Dev travelled to Idar with Shri Saubhagyabhai, one of His most devoted disciples. They stayed for about 10 days. During this visit, the King of Idar came to meet Param Krupalu Dev — a testament to the regard in which He was held even by royalty.

This visit proved to be one of the last significant meetings between Param Krupalu Dev and Saubhagyabhai. Shortly after returning from Idar, on Jeth Vad 10, V.S. 1953 (1897 AD), Saubhagyabhai passed away at 10:50 AM on a Thursday. At the exact moment of Saubhagyabhai's passing, Param Krupalu Dev intuitively took a cold water bath — indicating that He knew of the event through inner perception.

› Spiritual retreat (V.S. 1955)

On Magsar Sud 5, V.S. 1955 (1899 AD), Param Krupalu Dev left for Idar for what would become His most intensive retreat. He stayed at the residence of Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta, who was the Chief Medical Officer of Idar State and also the brother of Param Krupalu Dev's wife Jhabakbai.

During His time in Idar, Param Krupalu Dev explored the hills, forests, and caves in the surrounding area. He meditated extensively in ancient caves, including the Chandan caves, and spent six weeks in one such cave in a state of deep contemplation. His spiritual state during this period was one of intense dispassion and austerity, and His body showed visible signs of extreme vairagya.

› Teaching and renunciation

During the Idar retreat, monks visited Param Krupalu Dev for five days. He taught them the Bruhad Dravyasangrah, seated on the rocks known as 'Pudhvishila' and 'Siddhashila' — names evocative of the Jain cosmological places of liberation.

At a gathering during this period, Param Krupalu Dev formally proclaimed His renunciation of wealth, wife, and son — a definitive turning point marking His complete withdrawal from worldly life. By the end of V.S. 1955 (1899 AD), He had willed His entire business assets to His brother Mansukhbhai and completely retired from commerce.

It was also during this time that Param Krupalu Dev was overheard telling Devkaranji Muni about His own spiritual progression — a rare and deeply significant disclosure. Shri Thakarshi, nephew of Saubhagyabhai, was also present during this retreat.
