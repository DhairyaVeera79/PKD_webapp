Vavania (also called Vavania-mota) is a village in the Morbi district of Saurashtra, Gujarat. It is the birthplace of Shrimad Rajchandraji, who was born here on Kartik Purnima, V.S. 1924 (November 10, 1867 AD). Param Krupalu Dev's grandfather Shri Panchanbhai moved here from Manekwada in V.S. 1892 and bought a house. Param Krupalu Dev's father Shri Ravjibhai, born V.S. 1902, ran a moneylending business in Vavania and the nearby village of Chamanpar. At the time, Vavania was a thriving port with cargo ships sailing abroad — it was known as a "Bandar" (harbour). A peace treaty between Kutch and Morbi over Vavania's bay waters (around V.S. 1923, while Param Krupalu Dev was in His mother's womb) restored the harbour and trade flourished. Param Krupalu Dev spent His childhood and early education here until around age 13 (V.S. 1937), when He left for Morbi for further studies. Many of the most significant events of His early life — jatismaranjnan, extraordinary intellectual feats, and His first literary compositions — took place in this village.

› Birth of Param Krupalu Dev

Vavania, Saurashtra — birthplace of Shrimad Rajchandraji.

Shrimad Rajchandraji was born as Laxminandan on Kartik Purnima (Devdiwali), Sunday, at 2:00 AM, V.S. 1924 — corresponding to November 10, 1867 AD — in Vavania. He was the second child of Shri Ravjibhai, who followed the Vaishnav tradition, and Smt. Devba, who came from a Sthanakvasi Jain family of Maliya. This unique confluence of both traditions influenced His early upbringing — He wore a kanthi (Vaishnav beaded necklace given by Shri Ramdasji), visited the Ram Mandir, and also watched His mother perform Pratikraman. Both spiritual streams flowed through Him from childhood.

Before His birth, Yogini Rambaiba of Vavania had predicted that Devbai would bear an extraordinary son. A fakir whom Ravjibhai had served also made a similar prophecy. His birth fell on the same tithi as that of Kalikaalsarvajna Acharyashri Hemchandrasuriji (V.S. 1145, approximately 800 years earlier).

› Name change of Param Krupalu Dev

In V.S. 1928 (~1872 AD), at the age of about 4, Param Krupalu Dev's name was changed from Laxminandan to Raichand (Sanskrit: Rajchandra). While the exact reason for the name change is not documented in the book, the significance is profound — in many of His recollected past births, His name had been Raichand. In His immediately preceding birth, He had been a Rajkumar (prince) in Nepal/Tibet and had two wives. One of those wives was reborn as Shri Juthabhai in this life, and the other as Chanchalben. In the birth prior to that, He had been a Digambar Sadhu in Idar, an extraordinary scholar of Jainism who had been poisoned. Knowing that He was poisoned, He assumed the kayotsarg posture and left His body in meditation.

› Jatismaran Jnan

In V.S. 1931 (1875 AD), at the age of 7, Shri Amichandbhai of Vavania died of a snakebite. The young Raichand witnessed His cremation at the village's burning ground. At the sight of the burning pyre, He experienced jatismaranjnan — the spontaneous recollection of His past lives. The book records that He recalled details of numerous past births.

This transformative experience was the pivotal event of His childhood. From this moment, the awareness of the soul's eternal, transmigrating nature became His lived reality, not merely an intellectual concept. It fundamentally altered the course of His life and intensified His spiritual seeking.

› Schooling

Param Krupalu Dev enrolled in the local school in Vavania in V.S. 1931 (1875 AD), at age 7. His teacher Shri Lavjibhai was astonished by His instant mastery of numbers, multiplication tables, the alphabet, and lessons. He showed extraordinary grasping power and memory — completing the Gujarati curriculum equivalent to 7 standards in just 2 years.

At age 8 (~V.S. 1932), He had already written approximately 5,000 couplets (not currently available). At age 9, He studied the Ramayana and Mahabharata and wove them into verse. By age 10, He could deliver engaging speeches on many subjects. At age 11 (~V.S. 1935), He wrote mature articles published in the 'Buddhiprakash' magazine and won prizes for essays. At age 12 (~V.S. 1936), He wrote 300 couplets on a clock in just 3 days.

His mamas (maternal uncles) staying in Rajkot recommended that He study law, given His extraordinary intelligence. They also suggested English education in Rajkot — an opportunity that Gandhiji would later receive at Alfred High School — since no English school existed in Vavania. However, Param Krupalu Dev was not interested, as He felt it would make Him outward-looking (bahirmukh), whereas He wished to pursue His soul's welfare (kalyan). He had observed how Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta, upon returning from medical studies abroad, had his mannerisms, food habits, and attitudes changed — and how it took time for him to return to the Indian way of life.

Around V.S. 1937 (~1881 AD), at age 13, He left Vavania for Morbi to continue His studies. By this time, He had become fully coloured by Jainism. The kanthi (Vaishnav beaded necklace) given by Shri Ramdasji broke and was never worn again. He had read all the Jain Agams (canonical texts) in just 15 months and began intense study of scriptures related to detachment and truth.

› First Composition

Param Krupalu Dev's first literary composition was a poem written at the age of 8, in V.S. 1932 (~1876 AD). By this young age, He had written approximately 5,000 couplets. This early work demonstrated the extraordinary spiritual and literary maturity that would characterise His prolific output throughout His life — culminating in masterworks like Mokshmala (age 16) and Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra (age 28).

› Temple Construction

Approximately 45 years after Param Krupalu Dev's passing in Rajkot (V.S. 1957 / 1901 AD), His first daughter Javalben and her husband Shri Bhagvanbhai Modi (who was from Dharampur) purchased land in Vavania and constructed a temple at the very location where Param Krupalu Dev had been born. Brahmachariji Shri Govardhandasji performed the pratishtha (consecration) of Param Krupalu Dev's murti in the temple. The room of Param Krupalu Dev's birth and His cradle were preserved within the temple complex.

› In current times

The room in which Param Krupalu Dev was born, along with His cradle, were preserved within the temple complex. However, during the devastating Bhuj earthquake of 2001, the structure suffered severe cracks. The temple building was in danger of collapse and had to be temporarily closed for darshan. Renovation was subsequently carried out, but the building lost its original character and form — the house had originally been C-shaped.
