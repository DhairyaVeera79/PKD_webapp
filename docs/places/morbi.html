Morbi

Morbi city skyline
Morbi
Region Saurashtra, Gujarat, India
Significance Schooling, Avdhans, Marriage of Shrimad Rajchandraji
Period V.S. 1937–1944 (1881–1888 AD)
Key events Anglo-Vernacular schooling, First Avdhans, Marriage to Jhabakbai, Composition of Mokshmala

Morbi (also known as Morvi) is a city and former princely state in the Saurashtra region of Gujarat. It is situated on the Machhu River, approximately 50 km from Vavania and 60 km from Rajkot. Jetpar, a suburb of Morbi, is where Param Krupalu Dev's eldest sister Shivkunvarben married Shri Chatrabhuj Bechar. Param Krupalu Dev's Foiba (Smt. Janbai) also resided in Morbi. Morbi was a significant trading town, and it was here that Param Krupalu Dev's fame began to spread — being a large town, news from Morbi carried far. He came here around V.S. 1937 (~1881 AD) at age 13 for further education, and some of the most defining events of His youth took place here: the composition of Mokshmala, the beginning of His extraordinary avdhan demonstrations, and His marriage to Jhabakbai.

› Composition of Mokshmala

Main article: Mokshmala
Morbi, Saurashtra — where Param Krupalu Dev composed Mokshmala.

In V.S. 1940 (1884 AD), at the age of 16 years and 5 months, Param Krupalu Dev composed Mokshmala in just 3 days at the house of Shri Vinaychandrabhai Popatbhai Daftari, during the month of Chaitra. The work contains 108 chapters (pads) drawing from the Jain Agams (especially the Uttaradhyayan Sutra), philosophical discussions, and guidance on right conduct. It is organized in three parts: Samyag Darshan, Samyag Jnan, and Samyag Charitra. The chapters are remarkably concise. It was published later in V.S. 1944 in Ahmedabad, with Pujyashri Juthabhai assisting in its publication.

› Avdhans started

Main article: Avdhans

In V.S. 1940 (~1884 AD), at age 16, Param Krupalu Dev witnessed Shastri Shankarlal Maheshwar Bhatt perform 8 avdhans (ashtavdhan) in Morbi. The family had taken Him to observe, hoping He would be inspired. The very next day, Param Krupalu Dev performed ashtavdhan himself among friends in Vasant Baug — for Him, it did not seem a particularly difficult task. He had not known about avdhan until the previous day, yet by merely watching once He could replicate and surpass the feat. Word spread quickly: a 16-year-old child had performed ashtavdhan that had taken a seasoned scholar years of preparation.

Two days later, He performed 12 avdhans in Morbi's sacred Jain shelter (upashray) before an audience of 2,000 people. This launched a series of avdhan demonstrations across Saurashtra and Gujarat — in Jamnagar (where He received the title 'The Jewel of Hind'), Wadhwan, Botad, Jetpar, Ahmedabad, and culminating in the famous Shatavdhan in Mumbai.

› First Chitrapat

Main article: Chitrapats
First Chitrapat of Param Krupalu Dev.

The first chitrapat (photograph) of Param Krupalu Dev was taken here, around age 16. He wore a Jamnagar-style turban, and the photograph was probably taken at a studio in Morbi. This cherished photograph was later kept by Pujyashri Lalluji Muni and Pujyashri Ambalalbhai.

› Marriage with Jhabakbai

Param Krupalu Dev married Jhabakbai, daughter of Shri Popatlalbhai Mehta, on Maha Sud 12, V.S. 1944 (1888 AD) in Morbi, at the age of 20. The Jagjivan family had come to know of Param Krupalu Dev through His avdhan demonstrations in Morbi and wished that the eldest brother's daughter should marry this great soul. Param Krupalu Dev had received even better offers from other families because of His growing fame across Gujarat, but He chose this match, saying: "Purnanu runanu bandh chhe" (there are karmic bonds with her from past lives to be closed).

During the wedding fera (circumambulation), around the 3rd round, someone asked which number fera was this. Param Krupalu Dev replied: "Ame fero farva nahi, fero taalva aavya chhiye." (We have come not to take rounds, but to end the cycle of rounds.) While returning from the wedding, saffron water (kesar) rained on His vehicle — He was bestowed the title 'Yugpradhan'. According to tradition, when someone destined to be a Yugpurush gets married, kesar rain occurs. There is now a temple at the location of the marriage.

› Later visits to Morbi

After His marriage in V.S. 1944, Param Krupalu Dev left for Mumbai to pursue His career in the jewellery business and did not reside in Morbi again. However, He returned on notable occasions.

In V.S. 1954 (1898 AD), Param Krupalu Dev came back to Morbi and stayed for about three months. During this time, He delivered a series of spiritual discourses that were attended by many seekers. These sermons were later compiled and published as Vyakhyansaar-1, one of the important compilations of His spoken teachings.

In V.S. 1956 (1900 AD), Param Krupalu Dev visited Morbi again before proceeding to Wadhwan, where He would undertake some of His most important spiritual work.
