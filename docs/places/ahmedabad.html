Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad was one of the most important cities in Param Krupalu Dev's life. He first came here in V.S. 1944 (1888 AD) to publish Mokshmala, during which He met Pujyashri Juthabhai — a transformative spiritual connection. He performed avdhans at Sheth Dalpatbhai Bhagubhai's compound, returned multiple times for spiritual visits, and in V.S. 1957, during His final days, stayed for 27 days at the Aga Khan's bungalow on the Sabarmati River, where Shri Ambalalbhai attained self-realisation through Param Krupalu Dev's teachings.

› Printing of Mokshmala and first meeting with Pujyashri Juthabhai

Main article: Mokshmala; Pujyashri Juthabhai
The peacock emblem of Ahmedabad, representing the city's name meaning "abode of peacocks".

In V.S. 1944 (1888 AD), at age 20, Param Krupalu Dev did not have the funds to publish Mokshmala. He collected some sponsorship — Nancybhai of Veraval contributed — and decided to come to Ahmedabad, where printing was cheaper than in Bombay. However, He did not know anyone in the city.

Shri Vinaychand Popatbhai Daftari wrote a letter of recommendation addressed to Shri Jaisinghbhai Ujamsingh, the elder brother of Pujyashri Juthabhai, describing the bearer as a remarkable scholar and Shatavdhani (a title that carried great prestige at the time) and requesting any assistance possible. When Param Krupalu Dev arrived with this letter, Jaisinghbhai, occupied with his business, directed his younger brother Juthabhai to help.

Thus began Param Krupalu Dev's historic association with Pujyashri Juthabhai. Though Juthabhai lived only two more years (he passed away on Ashadh Sud 9, V.S. 1946 / 1890 AD, at the age of 23), the spiritual transformation he underwent in Param Krupalu Dev's company was extraordinary — he attained Samyak Darshan (right faith). Param Krupalu Dev stayed in Ahmedabad for 2 to 2.5 months for the printing of Mokshmala.

› Avdhans in Ahmedabad

Main article: Avdhans

During His V.S. 1944 (1888 AD) visit to Ahmedabad for the printing of Mokshmala, Param Krupalu Dev also demonstrated His avdhan powers. He performed avdhans at the compound of Sheth Shri Dalpatbhai Bhagubhai in Ahmedabad. These demonstrations further spread His fame across Gujarat and drew many admirers and seekers to Him.

› Later visits to Ahmedabad

In Kartik V.S. 1945 (1889 AD), Param Krupalu Dev visited Ahmedabad and stayed at Pujyashri Juthabhai's residence in Cheepa Pol for 15 days. During the same year, Shri Ambalalbhai and Shri Chhotalalbhai had come to Ahmedabad for a wedding and met Juthabhai, through whom Ambalalbhai became deeply inspired.

In V.S. 1956 (1900 AD), Param Krupalu Dev visited Ahmedabad again during the final phase of His life, as His health was declining. He also visited nearby Viramgam before proceeding to Vavania and Morbi.

› Ambalalbhai's self-realisation

In the final period of His life, around Kartak Vad 7, V.S. 1957 (~1900–1901), Param Krupalu Dev left Wadhwan Camp and came to Ahmedabad, where He stayed for 27 days at the Aga Khan's bungalow on the Sabarmati River. Despite His severely deteriorating health, Param Krupalu Dev continued to shower unprecedented spiritual wisdom on His closest disciples.

During this stay, He wrote 12 vows for His mother Devba and sent her along with wife Jhabakbai to take vows from monks. Most significantly, it was during this Ahmedabad stay that Shri Ambalalbhai attained Shuddh Samyagdarshan (self-realisation) through what is described as Param Krupalu Dev's "unprecedented showers of wisdom." This was also the time of Param Krupalu Dev's last meeting with Pujyashri Lalluji Muni and Devkaranji Muni, during which He told them: "Do not differentiate between me and the Dispassionate Lord."
