Kavitha

Kavitha village, Charotar region, Gujarat
Kavitha (Kawitha)
Region Charotar, Gujarat (near Nadiad)
Significance Site of Param Krupalu Dev's first major spiritual retreat; earliest outward signs of deep renunciation
Period V.S. 1952 & 1954 (1896 & 1898 AD)
Key locations Jhaverchand Sheth's residence; Vagadia lake; Mithuji's well; Mahadev Mandir
Key events Paryushan retreat; nightly meditations by Vagadia lake; incident with bandit Babardeva; discourses on Thanang Sutra

Kavitha (also spelt Kawitha) is a village in Gujarat's Charotar region, near Nadiad. It was the site of Shrimad Rajchandra's first major spiritual retreat in Shravan V.S. 1952 (1896 AD) — a turning point that marked the beginning of His transition from householder life to full renunciation. Param Krupalu Dev stayed at the residence of Shri Jhaverchand Sheth, spending His days in solitary meditation at Vagadia lake, Mithuji's well, Bhaidva well, and the surrounding fields. He returned for a second retreat in Shravan V.S. 1954 (1898 AD), during which He delivered discourses on the Shri Thanang Sutra, Yoga Vasishtha, and Vicharsagar. These retreats in Kavitha showed the earliest outward signs of Param Krupalu Dev's deep and intensifying renunciation.

› First retreat (V.S. 1952)

In Shravan V.S. 1952 (1896 AD), Param Krupalu Dev left Mumbai for the Charotar district of Gujarat, beginning a period of intensive spiritual retreats. Kavitha was the first significant stop on this journey. He stayed at the residence of Shri Jhaverchand Sheth, a devoted householder.

During this retreat, Param Krupalu Dev spent Paryushan (the annual Jain period of austerity and reflection) in the village. He meditated at various solitary spots — Mithuji's well, Bhaidva well, and Vajigorani's pastureland in the outlying fields. A caretaker named Shri Lallubhai attended to His basic needs during this period.

Among those who served Param Krupalu Dev during the Kavitha retreat were Shri Kunwarjibhai, Shri Laherabhai, Shri Vanmalibhai, Shri Jhaverbhai, and Shri Vrajbhai Gangadas Patel. Shamalbhai, another attendant, would bring jasmine flowers for Param Krupalu Dev.

› Nightly meditations

Param Krupalu Dev's practice during the Kavitha retreat was characterised by long nightly meditations. He would walk alone to the Vagadia lake after dark and sit in deep meditation by its banks, sometimes for hours. These solitary nocturnal meditations alarmed His companions, who on occasion formed search parties with lanterns to find Him when He did not return.

Shri Ratanchand and Shri Venichand were among those who went searching for Param Krupalu Dev with lanterns during such episodes. On one occasion, Param Krupalu Dev wandered alone through the dark of night far from the village, completely absorbed in His inner state — showing the depth of His withdrawal from ordinary worldly awareness.

› Incident with Babardeva

A notable incident occurred during the Kavitha retreat involving a bandit named Babardeva. Babardeva was a feared figure in the area, known for his violent and lawless ways. When he encountered Param Krupalu Dev, however, he was so moved by His spiritual presence and fearlessness that the encounter ended peacefully — a testament to the transformative power of Param Krupalu Dev's inner state even upon those far removed from spiritual life.

› Second retreat (V.S. 1954)

In Shravan V.S. 1954 (1898 AD), Param Krupalu Dev returned to Kavitha for a second retreat. By this time, His spiritual state had intensified considerably — He had long since attained self-realisation (V.S. 1947) and was now in the advanced stages of His spiritual evolution.

During this second visit, Param Krupalu Dev delivered discourses on several major scriptures: the Shri Thanang Sutra (a canonical Jain text), Yoga Vasishtha (a Hindu philosophical text on the nature of consciousness), and Vicharsagar (an Advaita Vedanta text). These teachings across traditions demonstrated Param Krupalu Dev's characteristic breadth — He drew from Jain, Vedantic, and yogic scriptures with equal mastery, always pointing towards the essential truth of self-realisation.
