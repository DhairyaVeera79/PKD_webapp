Rajkot, in the Saurashtra region of Gujarat, is the sacred site where Shrimad Rajchandra left His mortal body on Chaitra Vad 5, V.S. 1957 (April 9, 1901). He arrived in Rajkot from Wadhwan in Fagan V.S. 1957, gravely ill but in a state of profound inner composure. His final days here, His last words, and His departure in a state of absolute samadhi are among the most deeply revered events in His life. The Samadhi Sthal (memorial) in Rajkot is a place of pilgrimage. Gandhiji celebrated Shrimad Rajchandra Jayanti here and, on his first visit to Rajkot after Param Krupalu Dev's death, went directly from the railway station to the memorial.

› Early connection with Rajkot

Param Krupalu Dev's maternal uncles (mamas) resided in Rajkot. During His childhood in Vavania, they observed His extraordinary intelligence and recommended that He pursue English education and a career in law. Rajkot had access to English schooling — notably Alfred High School, where Gandhiji later studied — and the mamas felt that Param Krupalu Dev's intellect would be well served there. However, Param Krupalu Dev declined, recognising that such pursuits would make Him outward-focused (bahirmukh) when His true aspiration was the welfare of the soul (Atma kalyan).

› Final journey to Rajkot

By V.S. 1956 (1900 AD), Param Krupalu Dev's body had been struck by an incurable disease — first appearing as weakness and later diagnosed as colitis. Despite this, He continued His spiritual work with unwavering composure. After spending time in Dharampur, Ahmedabad, Vavania, Morbi, and Wadhwan, His body had deteriorated severely — reduced from 130–140 pounds to merely 57 pounds.

On Fagan Sud 6, V.S. 1957 (~1901 AD), Param Krupalu Dev left Wadhwan Camp and arrived in Rajkot, where He would spend His remaining days. He stayed in the Brahmakshatriya Mohalla area. Even in His final weeks, He continued to compose spiritual works — on Chaitra Sud 9, V.S. 1957, He composed His final poem, which stands as His last written message to the world.

› Dehvilay

On the evening of Chaitra Vad 4 (April 8, 1901), Param Krupalu Dev spoke His last words to those near Him — His brother Mansukhbhai, Revashankarbhai, and Narbherambhai: "Do not worry, this Soul is eternal."

At 2:30 AM on Chaitra Vad 5 (April 9, 1901, Tuesday), He caught a severe cold and told those present: "Your brother's death will be in the state of samadhi." At 7:30 AM, He instructed that He be transferred to a reclining couch to assume a meditative posture. At 7:45 AM, He accepted milk. At 8:45 AM, He spoke His final words: "Mansukh, do not be sad. Take care of mother. I am now abiding in the Self."

At 2:00 PM on Tuesday, Chaitra Vad 5, V.S. 1957 (April 9, 1901), Shrimad Rajchandra left His mortal body in Rajkot in a state of absolute samadhi.

His passing was covered by Mumbai Samachar, The Pioneer, The Indian Spectator, and other dailies of the time. On May 21, 1901, Gandhiji wrote to Revashankarbhai from Durban expressing his profound grief.

› Memorial (Samadhi Sthal)

A memorial (Samadhi Sthal) was built in Rajkot at the site associated with Param Krupalu Dev's final days. It became a place of pilgrimage for devotees and seekers. Gandhiji, who regarded Param Krupalu Dev as his greatest spiritual influence, celebrated Shrimad Rajchandra Jayanti at this memorial. On Gandhiji's first visit to Rajkot after Param Krupalu Dev's death, he went directly from the railway station to the Samadhi Sthal — such was his reverence.
