Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra

Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra
Composed Aso Vad 1, V.S. 1952 (~October 1896)
Age ~28 years
Duration Approximately 1.5 to 2 hours
Location Nadiad, Gujarat
Language Gujarati
Form Philosophical poem; 142 verses
Composed for Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai
Amanuensis Pujyashree Ambalalbhai (held lantern for light)
Theme Six fundamental truths (Shad Darshan) of spiritual philosophy
Significance Crown jewel of Param Krupalu Dev's compositions; encapsulates the essence of all scriptures

Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra (The Six Fundamentals / Self-Realisation) is the crown jewel of Shrimad Rajchandraji's compositions — a philosophical poem of 142 verses composed in approximately 1.5 to 2 hours on Aso Vad 1, V.S. 1952, at Nadiad in Gujarat. It was composed for Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai, Param Krupalu Dev's divine soulmate, while Pujyashree Ambalalbhai served as amanuensis, holding a lantern to provide light as he wrote down Param Krupalu Dev's dictation. The work establishes six fundamental truths through a dialogue between a Guru (enlightened preceptor) and a Shishya (seeker), and is regarded as encapsulating the essence of all scriptures. Pujya Gurudevshri Rakeshji's Ph.D. thesis from Mumbai University (1997) was on this very work.

› Composition

On the night of Aso Vad Ekam, V.S. 1952 (~October 1896), Shrimad Rajchandraji composed the entire Atmasiddhi Shastra in a single sitting of approximately 1.5 to 2 hours at Nadiad. The sheer speed and depth of the composition is extraordinary — 142 verses of intricate spiritual philosophy flowing without pause, correction, or revision. Pujyashree Ambalalbhai wrote down the verses as Param Krupalu Dev dictated them, holding a lantern in one hand to provide light during the nighttime composition.

The work was composed for Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai Lallubhai, Param Krupalu Dev's closest spiritual companion whom He called 'Hridayroop' (one who is the form of the heart). It arose from the context of Saubhagyabhai's deep spiritual yearning and the discussions within the group of devotees stationed at Nadiad.

› Structure & Six Fundamentals

The Atmasiddhi Shastra takes the form of a dialogue between a Guru (enlightened preceptor) and a Shishya (seeker). Through this dialogue, six fundamental truths (Shad Darshan) are systematically established:

  1. The soul exists (Atma chhe)
  2. The soul is eternal (Te nit chhe)
  3. The soul is the doer of its actions (Karta)
  4. The soul is the receiver of consequences (Bhokta)
  5. Liberation exists (Moksha chhe)
  6. The path to liberation exists (Te nu karan, samakit te hoy)

The opening verse captures the essence: 'Aatma chhe, te nit chhe, karta bhokta soy; moksha chhe, te nu karan, samakit te hoy' — affirming the existence, eternality, agency, and liberability of the soul, along with the path of right faith as the means.

› The occasion of composition

By V.S. 1952, Param Krupalu Dev was 28 years old and had reached the peak of His spiritual attainment. He was surrounded by earnest seekers including Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai, Pujyashree Ambalalbhai, and others who were stationed at Nadiad. The spiritual atmosphere was charged with intense longing and readiness.

Saubhagyabhai, despite being decades older than Param Krupalu Dev and already deeply advanced in practice, had an unquenchable thirst for the ultimate truth. The Atmasiddhi Shastra emerged as Param Krupalu Dev's definitive response — a work that distilled the essence of all philosophies into a single, self-contained masterpiece. It may be seen as the culmination of the entire Guru-Shishya relationship between Param Krupalu Dev and His devotees.

› Significance

The Atmasiddhi Shastra is universally regarded as the crown jewel of Param Krupalu Dev's literary and spiritual legacy. It encapsulates in 142 verses what countless volumes of scriptural literature elaborate upon. The extraordinary circumstances of its composition — flowing effortlessly in approximately 1.5 hours without a single correction — testify to Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary spiritual and creative capacity.

The work transcends sectarian boundaries. While grounded in the Jain philosophical tradition, its universal treatment of the soul, karma, and liberation speaks to seekers across all backgrounds. Pujya Gurudevshri Rakeshji chose this very work as the subject of His doctoral thesis at Mumbai University in 1997, further establishing its academic and spiritual importance in the modern era.
