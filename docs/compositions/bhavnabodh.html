Bhavnabodh

Bhavnabodh
Also known as Understanding of Reflections
Composed V.S. 1942 (1886 AD)
Age 18 years
Language Gujarati
Form Prose booklet; the twelve bhavnas, with illustrative examples
Theme Twelve sentiments a sincere seeker should cultivate to develop detachment
Occasion Written for those who had reserved advance copies of Mokshmala, whose printing had been delayed

Bhavnabodh is a small prose booklet composed by Param Krupalu Dev in V.S. 1942 (1886 AD), at the age of 18. It lucidly explains the twelve bhavnas — the sentiments that a sincere seeker should cultivate and develop — and contains illustrative examples that invoke great dispassion in the reader.

› Composition

Param Krupalu Dev had written Mokshmala in V.S. 1940 and published it Himself. There was, however, a delay in its publication. To appease the buyers who had reserved their copies in advance, He wrote Bhavnabodh in V.S. 1942 and gifted them a copy of the new book along with their order for the previous one.

His response to the delay in printing attests to His judiciousness, acumen and moral responsibility towards others — He chose to compose an entirely new work rather than let those who had trusted Him go unrewarded for their patience.

› The twelve bhavnas

The book lucidly explains the twelve bhavnas — reflections or sentiments that a sincere seeker should cultivate and develop. It is described as a small booklet inspiring the seeker to cultivate twelve specific sentiments leading to detachment from the material world.

Rather than treating them as abstract doctrine, Param Krupalu Dev presents the bhavnas through interesting examples that invoke great dispassion within the reader — the same approach to teaching through illustration that characterises Mokshmala.

› A surge of detachment

Param Krupalu Dev's detachment increased progressively through this period. During the creation of Mokshmala, He had experienced a state of detachment such that He would not even remember whether or not He had eaten. By the time He composed Bhavnabodh, that detachment had reached such a crescendo that it finds a mention in His autobiographical poem, which immortalises the main incidents of His life:

“1942, adorned with a surge of extraordinary detachment.”

› Significance

Bhavnabodh gives a systematic treatment of the twelve contemplations of the Jain tradition in simple, accessible language. Composed when Param Krupalu Dev was just 18, it belongs to the same early period as Mokshmala — works He wrote deliberately in plain language so that children and young readers would not be waylaid by erroneous beliefs.
