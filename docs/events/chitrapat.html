Significance Among the most circulated images of Param Krupalu Dev

Very few photographs of Param Krupalu Dev exist, making each chitrapat (photograph) of immense historical and spiritual value. The earliest known photograph was taken in Morbi during His youth, while the most widely circulated images were taken much later at Wadhwan Camp in V.S. 1956 — when His body was in a state of extreme austerity and dispassion. These photographs capture not merely His physical form but, as He Himself remarked, reflect His profound inner spiritual state.

› First Chitrapat

The first known photograph of Param Krupalu Dev was taken in Morbi around V.S. 1940 (~1884 AD), when He was approximately 16 years old. This was during the period of His early fame from avdhan demonstrations across Gujarat. At this young age, He had already authored Mokshmala and was becoming widely recognised as a prodigy of extraordinary abilities.

› Wadhwan Camp Photographs

The most famous and widely circulated photographs of Param Krupalu Dev were taken at Wadhwan Camp in V.S. 1956 (~1900 AD). These were requested by Mumukshu Shri Sukhlal Chaganlal of Viramgam. At this time, Param Krupalu Dev was in a state of extreme vairagya (dispassion), staying at the Limbdi State guest house during what would be one of His final periods of residence.

Two photographs were taken — one in lotus posture (padmasana) and one in a standing meditative pose. These images capture the extraordinary spiritual radiance that emanated from Him even as His physical body was deeply emaciated from prolonged fasting and austerities.

Later, Param Krupalu Dev told Mansukhbhai Devshi that the photographs captured His state akin to Shri Parshwanath during Kamath's torment — a reference to the 23rd Tirthankara's absolute equanimity while being subjected to terrible torment by the demon Kamath. This comparison indicated the depth of His inner composure and detachment despite the body's extreme condition.

› Significance

The Wadhwan Camp photographs remain among the most widely circulated images of Param Krupalu Dev and hold deep spiritual significance for devotees. They are not merely historical records but are regarded as windows into an elevated spiritual state — capturing a moment when the soul's luminosity was visibly reflected in the physical form. These chitrapats are venerated at ashrams and spiritual centres associated with Param Krupalu Dev's legacy, including Shrimad Rajchandra Ashram, Agas and Shrimad Rajchandra Mission Dharampur.
