Avdhans

Avdhans
Type Performance / Concentration demonstration
Period V.S. 1937-1946 (~1881-1890 AD)
Age ~13 to ~22 years
Highest feat Shatavdhan (100 simultaneous tasks)
Key venue Framjee Cawasjee Institute, Mumbai
Significance Demonstrated extraordinary concentration; later renounced as obstacle to self-realisation

Avadhanam (literally "concentration") is a performance art in India where a performer simultaneously handles multiple tasks — answering questions, composing poetry, playing games, solving mathematical calculations — all at once. Between V.S. 1937 and 1946 (~1881-1890 AD), Param Krupalu Dev astounded audiences across Gujarat and Mumbai with increasingly extraordinary avdhan demonstrations, culminating in the legendary Shatavdhan (100 simultaneous tasks) at the Framjee Cawasjee Institute. Though these feats brought Him immense fame — earning titles like "Hind na Heera" (The Jewel of India) and "Sakshat Saraswati" — He ultimately renounced these powers, calling them "tinsel" compared to the "gold" of spiritual attainment.

› Start of Avdhans

In approximately V.S. 1937 (~1881 AD), at around age 13, Param Krupalu Dev's family took Him to see Shastri Shankarlal Maheshwar Bhatt perform Ashtavdhan (8 simultaneous tasks) in Morbi. The intention was to inspire the young Raichand and make Him more alert and worldly-wise.

What happened next astonished everyone. The very next day, Param Krupalu Dev performed Ashtavdhan Himself in Vasant Baug among His friends — having never practised or even known about avdhans until the day before. For Him, it did not seem like a major or difficult task. Word spread quickly that a young boy had matched what an experienced performer had demonstrated. While Shankarlal Bhatt was older and had likely prepared extensively, Param Krupalu Dev accomplished the same feat spontaneously, simply by observing it once.

Just two days later, He performed 12 avdhans in the Upashray (Jain sacred shelter) in Morbi before an audience of 2,000 people. This stunning demonstration by a youth of barely 16 years established Him as a prodigy of extraordinary concentration.

› Avdhans across Gujarat

News of the young prodigy's abilities spread rapidly across Gujarat. From V.S. 1940 to 1945 (~1884-1889 AD), Param Krupalu Dev demonstrated avdhans in towns and cities across the region:

  • Jamnagar — Performed 12 and 16 avdhans. The audience, deeply impressed, bestowed upon Him the title "Hind na Heera" (The Jewel of India).
  • Wadhwan Camp (present-day Surendranagar) — Performed 16 avdhans before 2,000 people, including Colonel H. L. Natsaheb. Covered by newspapers including Mumbai Samachar, Lokmitra, and Nyayadarshak.
  • Limbdi — Performed 36 avdhans. Here He was awarded the title "Sakshat Saraswati" (The Goddess of Knowledge Incarnate).
  • Botad — Performed 52 avdhans in the presence of Sheth Shri Harilal Shivlal.
  • Ahmedabad — Performed 52 avdhans at the compound of Shri Dalpatbhai Bhagubhai (V.S. 1945).
  • Jetpar and Rajkot — Also demonstrated avdhan powers.

These demonstrations were extensively covered in both Gujarati and English newspapers, making Param Krupalu Dev a known figure across western India while still a teenager.

› Theosophical Society, Mumbai

Param Krupalu Dev was invited to perform avdhans at the Theosophical Society in Girgaum, Mumbai, at the age of 18 in V.S. 1942 (~1886 AD). This was a significant venue as the Theosophical Society attracted intellectuals and scholars interested in Eastern philosophy and spiritual traditions. He performed avdhans involving 9 languages simultaneously. Shri Rustamji Ardeshar Master presented Him with a garland and bouquet in recognition of His extraordinary abilities.

› Shatavdhan at Framjee Cawasjee Institute

The pinnacle of Param Krupalu Dev's avdhan demonstrations took place on Maha Vad 13, V.S. 1943 (January 22, 1887 AD, Saturday) at the Framjee Cawasjee Institute in Dhobi Talao, Mumbai. At the age of 19, He performed the legendary Shatavdhan — simultaneously keeping track of 100 different tasks while answering questions, composing poetry, playing games, and solving mathematical problems.

The event was presided over by Dr. Peterson, a European scholar, who was so deeply impressed that he awarded Param Krupalu Dev a gold medal. The performance created a media sensation, with British and Indian newspapers alike praising this extraordinary young man.

Among the distinguished attendees was Shri Maneklal Ghelabhai, a prominent jeweller from Vadodara. Deeply impressed, he later conversed with Param Krupalu Dev, who mentioned He needed to find a way to earn a livelihood. Maneklal Ghelabhai offered to teach Him jewellery valuation (zaverat), which later formed the basis of Param Krupalu Dev's business career.

Following the Shatavdhan, Sir Charles Sergeant, the Chief Magistrate of the Mumbai High Court, invited Param Krupalu Dev to perform in London and Europe with all expenses sponsored. He declined this invitation.

› Renunciation of Avdhans

Around V.S. 1945-1946 (~1889-1890 AD), Param Krupalu Dev made the conscious decision to completely cease all public avdhan demonstrations. The very day after the Shatavdhan at Framjee Cawasjee Institute, tremendous vairagya (dispassion) arose within Him. Though people were showering Him with adulation and importance, He recognised that this was not the goal of His life. He simply wanted to be a devoted seeker of the soul.

He later described these extraordinary powers as mere "tinsel" compared to the "gold" of spiritual attainment. He felt the avdhans had served their purpose — establishing His credibility and demonstrating that remarkable spiritual capacities existed — but were now a distraction from the ultimate goal of self-realisation. He expressed that these were worldly feats unworthy of a soul seeking liberation.

He declined invitations from London and Europe where everything was to be sponsored, choosing instead to turn completely inward toward his spiritual pursuit.

› Informal Avdhans

Even after ceasing public avdhan performances, Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary powers of concentration continued to manifest naturally in His daily life. He could simultaneously dictate letters to multiple scribes on different subjects, keep track of complex business dealings while engrossed in spiritual contemplation, and answer questions on diverse philosophical topics without any break in thought. These abilities were inherent to His nature rather than practised feats, and they continued to be witnessed by close associates throughout His life.

Notably, Shri Virchand Raghavji Gandhi, who represented the Jain religion at the Parliament of World's Religions in Chicago (September 1893), cited Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary capabilities — particularly His Shatavdhan and Jatismaranjnan — before the world assembly, bringing international attention to His genius.
