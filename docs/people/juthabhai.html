Pujyashree Juthabhai Ujamshibhai

Pujyashree Juthabhai Ujamshibhai
Pujyashree Juthabhai
Born Kartik Sud 2, V.S. 1923
Passed away Ashadh Sud 9, V.S. 1946 (age 23)
Residence Cheepa Pol, Ahmedabad
Relation First devoted disciple of Param Krupalu Dev
Title from Param Krupalu Dev 'Satyaparayan' (devoted to truth)
Family Sthanakvasi Jain
Letters from Param Krupalu Dev ~20 available
Significance First devotee; attained samyaktva (self-realisation)

Pujyashree Juthabhai Ujamshibhai was the first devoted disciple of Shrimad Rajchandraji, whom Param Krupalu Dev bestowed the title 'Satyaparayan' (devoted to truth). Born on Kartik Sud 2, V.S. 1923 — one year older than Param Krupalu Dev — Juthabhai belonged to a prominent Sthanakvasi Jain family in Ahmedabad. He met Param Krupalu Dev in V.S. 1944 during the publication of Mokshmala and instantly recognised His extraordinary spiritual stature. Though their association lasted only about two years, Juthabhai attained samyaktva (self-realisation) during that time. His recognition of Param Krupalu Dev proved immensely beneficial — it was through him that both Ambalalbhai and Lalluji Muni later came into Param Krupalu Dev's fold. He passed away at the young age of 23 on Ashadh Sud 9, V.S. 1946.

› Background

Juthabhai was born into a religiously prominent family. His father was Shri Ujamshibhai and his mother was Jamnabai. His grandfather, Sheth Mallichand Jechand of Ahmedabad, was a figure of religious standing. Juthabhai's elder brother, Sheth Jesangbhai Ujamshibhai, was a successful businessman. Juthabhai's wife was Ugariben.

Despite belonging to a wealthy family, Juthabhai was deeply spiritual by nature and had studied up to the fourth standard in English. He resided in Cheepa Pol, Ahmedabad.

› First meeting with Param Krupalu Dev

In V.S. 1944 (1888 AD), twenty-year-old Param Krupalu Dev travelled to Ahmedabad to publish Mokshmala, as printing there was cheaper than in Mumbai. He did not know anyone in the city. Vanechand Popatbhai Daftary from Morbi gave Param Krupalu Dev a letter of recommendation addressed to Sheth Jesangbhai Ujamshibhai, Juthabhai's elder brother. The letter described Param Krupalu Dev as a remarkable scholar and Shatavdhani, and requested help for Him.

When Param Krupalu Dev arrived, Sheth Jesangbhai was preoccupied with his business and asked his younger brother Juthabhai to assist the young visitor instead. This is how Juthabhai and Param Krupalu Dev met for the first time. Juthabhai helped Param Krupalu Dev with the publication of Mokshmala through the press of Sheth Jesangbhai. He was often away on business, and so he entrusted Juthabhai to take care of Param Krupalu Dev's needs.

› Devotion and nature

Juthabhai was of an exceptionally tender and devotional nature. Those who knew him said he had a stree-hriday — a heart as soft as a woman's. When separated from Param Krupalu Dev, he would weep, refuse food and drink, lose sleep, and fall ill. News of his distress would reach Param Krupalu Dev, who would then travel to Ahmedabad to see him.

Juthabhai was also deeply dispassionate (vairagi) by nature. About him, Param Krupalu Dev said: "Jutho sansaar maate hato, baaki satyaparayan hato" — he was called Jutha (meaning false) only in worldly terms, but in truth he was utterly devoted to truth. He was Param Krupalu Dev's first fully surrendered (samarpit) disciple, as distinct from the many admirers (chahak shishya).

Param Krupalu Dev wrote approximately 20 letters to Juthabhai, many of which contain intimate spiritual guidance and reveal the depth of their bond.

› Attainment of samyaktva

Through his association with Param Krupalu Dev and his own intense devotion, Juthabhai attained samyaktva — right faith or self-realisation. His spiritual ripeness was such that despite their brief time together, Param Krupalu Dev considered his inner transformation complete.

› Passing

Param Krupalu Dev predicted Juthabhai's death two months before it occurred. He asked Shri Chaganlal Becharlal to inform Juthabhai about his imminent passing. Juthabhai passed away on Ashadh Sud 9, V.S. 1946, at the age of 23 — just about two years after meeting Param Krupalu Dev. His passing at such a young age was a source of grief for the entire circle of devotees, but Param Krupalu Dev's foreknowledge of it testified to His extraordinary spiritual powers.

› Legacy

Juthabhai's recognition of Param Krupalu Dev was of historic consequence. It was through Juthabhai that Ambalalbhai first encountered Param Krupalu Dev's letters and decided to meet Him. In V.S. 1945, at a wedding in Ahmedabad, Ambalalbhai met Juthabhai, who showed him Param Krupalu Dev's letters. So moved was Ambalalbhai that he later travelled to Mumbai with Tribhuvanbhai to meet Param Krupalu Dev in person.

Juthabhai's connection also indirectly led Lalluji Muni to Param Krupalu Dev's fold. In this way, the first devotee's recognition of the Master proved a gateway for two of the other greatest disciples.

Param Krupalu Dev also indicated that Juthabhai, along with Chanchalben, had been His wife in a previous birth as a prince in Nepal/Tibet, where His name was also Raichand.
