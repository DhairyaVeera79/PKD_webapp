Pujyashree Ambalalbhai Lalchand

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai
Born V.S. 1926 (two years younger than Param Krupalu Dev)
Passed away Chaitra Vad 12, V.S. 1963, age 37
Native place Khambhat
Father Shri Maganlalbhai (adopted by Shri Lalchandbhai)
Wife Parsanben
Letters from Param Krupalu Dev ~127
Self-realisation V.S. 1957, in Ahmedabad
Significance Bhakt Ratna; amanuensis of Atmasiddhi Shastra; compiled 'Shrimad Rajchandra'

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai Lalchand (originally Ambalal Maganlal) was one of the four illustrious Bhakt Ratnas of Shrimad Rajchandraji. Born in V.S. 1926 in Khambhat, he was two years younger than Param Krupalu Dev and served Him with tireless devotion. He held the lantern while Param Krupalu Dev composed the Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra in a single sitting at Nadiad, recorded His sermons verbatim, learnt Sanskrit at His behest, and ultimately compiled and published the monumental work 'Shrimad Rajchandra' along with Mansukhbhai. He attained self-realisation in V.S. 1957 in Ahmedabad, and passed away at the young age of 37, his last words being 'Sahajatmaswaroop Hey Prabhu'.

› Background

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai was born in Khambhat as the son of Shri Maganlalbhai. He was adopted by his maternal grandfather Shri Lalchandbhai, and hence came to be known as Ambalal Lalchand. His wife was Parsanben, and he had a younger brother, Shri Nagindas Maganlal (also known as Naginbhai). His close friends from Khambhat were Shri Chhotalalbhai, Shri Sundarlalbhai, and Shri Tribhuvanbhai — all sons of Shri Manekchandbhai.

› First meeting with Param Krupalu Dev

In Vaishakh of V.S. 1945, Pujyashree Ambalalbhai and Shri Chhotalalbhai attended a wedding in Ahmedabad where they met Pujyashree Juthabhai. Juthabhai showed them Param Krupalu Dev's letters, and Ambalalbhai was immediately inspired by the depth of spiritual wisdom they contained.

Subsequently, Ambalalbhai travelled to Mumbai with Tribhuvanbhai to meet Param Krupalu Dev in person. This meeting proved transformative — Ambalalbhai recognised Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary spiritual stature and devoted himself to His service from that point onward.

› Service to Param Krupalu Dev

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai served Param Krupalu Dev with extraordinary dedication. He recorded Param Krupalu Dev's sermons and discourses verbatim, preserving teachings that would have otherwise been lost. At Param Krupalu Dev's instruction, he learnt Sanskrit to better understand and transmit the scriptural knowledge. He composed the devotional verse 'Maha devyaha kukshiratnam...' in honour of Param Krupalu Dev.

Param Krupalu Dev wrote approximately 127 letters to Ambalalbhai, containing deep philosophical and instructional guidance. These letters form a significant portion of the spiritual literature that was eventually compiled into the posthumous work 'Shrimad Rajchandra'.

› Atmasiddhi Shastra

On Aso Vad 1, V.S. 1952 (~October 1896), Param Krupalu Dev composed the Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra — 142 verses in approximately 1.5 to 2 hours — at dusk in Nadiad, the day after Sharad Purnima. Pujyashree Ambalalbhai played a pivotal role in this historic moment: he served as the amanuensis, holding the lantern that illuminated the pages as Param Krupalu Dev wrote this masterwork in a single inspired sitting. This composition, written for Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai, is considered the pinnacle of Param Krupalu Dev's literary and spiritual contribution.

› Self-realisation

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai attained self-realisation in V.S. 1957 in Ahmedabad, through what was described as Param Krupalu Dev's "unprecedented showers of wisdom." Param Krupalu Dev disclosed this attainment to Shri Dharshibhai Kushalchand. Despite being told earlier by Param Krupalu Dev about his indolence as an obstacle, Ambalalbhai persevered with unwavering devotion and ultimately achieved the highest spiritual state.

› Compilation of 'Shrimad Rajchandra'

After Param Krupalu Dev's Dehvilay in V.S. 1957, Pujyashree Ambalalbhai undertook the monumental task of compiling Param Krupalu Dev's writings. Together with Shri Mansukhbhai (Param Krupalu Dev's brother), he published the first edition of 'Shrimad Rajchandra' in V.S. 1961. This work brought together approximately 920 letters, poems, prose, and philosophical writings of Param Krupalu Dev, preserving His teachings for future generations. Mahatma Gandhi later wrote the preface for the second edition of this compilation.

› Passing

Pujyashree Ambalalbhai passed away on Chaitra Vad 12, V.S. 1963, at the age of 37. He was in Khambhat, where he had contracted plague while selflessly serving Shri Chhotalal Kapurchand of Fenav. Even in his final moments, his consciousness was centred on the soul — his last words were 'Sahajatmaswaroop Hey Prabhu' (O Lord who is the natural form of the Self), a testament to the depth of his spiritual attainment.
