Pujyashree Lalluji Muni (Prabhushri)

Pujyashree Lalluji Muni
Born Aso Vad 1, V.S. 1910, Vataman (Bhal region, Gujarat)
Passed away Vaishakh Sud 8, V.S. 1992, 8:10 PM, in unprecedented samadhi
Other names Prabhushri; Shri Laghuraj Swami
Title from Param Krupalu Dev 'Muni of the fourth era'
Father Shri Krishnadas (passed away before his birth)
Mother Kasliba
Initiated as monk V.S. 1940, Khambhat
Letters from Param Krupalu Dev 92
Self-realisation V.S. 1954, last day in Vaso
Significance Bhakt Ratna; founded Agas Ashram; spread Param Krupalu Dev's message for decades

Pujyashree Lalluji Muni, also known as Prabhushri and Shri Laghuraj Swami, was one of the four Bhakt Ratnas of Shrimad Rajchandraji who called him the 'Muni of the fourth era'. Born on Aso Vad 1, V.S. 1910, in Vataman in the Bhal region of Gujarat to a reputed Bhavsar family, he was 14 years older than Param Krupalu Dev and already an initiated monk when he first met the young householder in Khambhat. Despite the difference in their outward circumstances, Lalluji Muni prostrated three times before Param Krupalu Dev upon their first meeting, recognising His extraordinary spiritual height. He attained self-realisation in V.S. 1954 through Param Krupalu Dev's grace, established Shrimad Rajchandra Ashram at Agas, and spent his entire life spreading Param Krupalu Dev's message. He departed in an unprecedented state of samadhi on Vaishakh Sud 8, V.S. 1992.

› Background

Pujyashree Lalluji Muni was born into a reputed Bhavsar family in Vataman, a village in the Bhal region of Gujarat. His father, Shri Krishnadas, had passed away before his birth, and he was raised by his mother Kasliba. He was the only child of four mothers. He married twice and had one son before renouncing the householder's life to adopt monkhood.

› Monastic life

In V.S. 1940, Lalluji Muni was initiated as a monk in the Sthanakvasi sect at Khambhat by Shri Harakhchandji Muni, along with his friend Shri Devkaranji. As a Sthanakvasi monk, he followed the traditional practices of the sect, which emphasised scriptural study and a simple, itinerant monastic life.

› First meeting with Param Krupalu Dev

The momentous first meeting between Pujyashree Lalluji Muni and Param Krupalu Dev took place in V.S. 1946, in Khambhat, on the auspicious day of Dhanteras, at the Sthanakvasi Upashray. Param Krupalu Dev had come at Pujyashree Ambalalbhai's request. Though Lalluji Muni was 14 years older than Param Krupalu Dev and an ordained monk while Param Krupalu Dev was a young householder, the monk prostrated three times before Him — an extraordinary act that testified to the spiritual authority he perceived in Param Krupalu Dev.

› Association with Param Krupalu Dev

In V.S. 1949, Lalluji Muni spent 4 monsoon months at the Upashray in Chinchpokli, Mumbai, visiting Param Krupalu Dev daily. In V.S. 1950, while stationed in Surat during the monsoon months, he earnestly requested self-realisation from Param Krupalu Dev. In response, Param Krupalu Dev sent him the celebrated 'Chha Padno Patra' (Patrank-493) — a letter in verse form containing six stanzas of profound spiritual instruction.

Param Krupalu Dev wrote 92 letters to Lalluji Muni, offering guidance on monastic practice, inner awakening, and the path to liberation. In these letters, Param Krupalu Dev addressed him with the highest reverence, calling him the 'Muni of the fourth era' — a title indicating that Lalluji Muni's spiritual stature was comparable to the great monks of ancient times.

› Self-realisation

The crowning moment came in Bhadarva V.S. 1954, during the retreat in Vaso where Lalluji Muni and other monks were stationed for the monsoon months. On the last day of their time together, Param Krupalu Dev spoke to Lalluji Muni for one hour, imparting a torrent of spiritual wisdom that completely transformed the monk's attachment into unwavering focus on the soul. In that single hour, Lalluji Muni attained self-realisation — a testament both to Param Krupalu Dev's power as a spiritual master and to Lalluji Muni's own readiness to receive.

Shri Dhoribhai sang hymns at Param Krupalu Dev's request during this retreat, and the atmosphere was charged with extraordinary spiritual energy.

› Establishment of Agas Ashram

On Jeth Sud 5, V.S. 1976 (~1920 AD), Pujyashree Lalluji Muni established Shrimad Rajchandra Ashram at Agas on 7.5 acres of farmland generously donated by Shri Jijibhai Kuberdas of Sandesar. This ashram became the primary centre for the study, preservation, and dissemination of Param Krupalu Dev's teachings. He spent 14 monsoon seasons at Agas Ashram, and it became a place of pilgrimage for seekers of truth.

› Legacy

Pujyashree Lalluji Muni dedicated his entire life to spreading Param Krupalu Dev's message. He travelled extensively, calling everyone 'Prabhu' (Lord), reflecting his vision of the divine soul within all beings. His key disciple was Brahmachariji Shri Govardhandasji, who acquired a very high spiritual state and wrote 'Shrimad Rajchandra Jeevankala', continuing the lineage of devotion and study that Lalluji Muni had established.

› Passing

Pujyashree Lalluji Muni departed on Vaishakh Sud 8, V.S. 1992, at 8:10 PM, at Agas Ashram. His passing is described as having taken place in an unprecedented samadhi — an extraordinarily peaceful and spiritually elevated departure that mirrored the samadhi-deaths of Param Krupalu Dev's other great devotees, Pujyashree Saubhagyabhai and Pujyashree Ambalalbhai.
