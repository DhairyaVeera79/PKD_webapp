Mahatma Gandhiji (Mohandas), about one and three-quarter years younger than Param Krupalu Dev, met Him in Jeth of V.S. 1947 at the home of Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta in Mumbai. Gandhiji had just returned from England as a barrister. During his two years practising law in Mumbai, he frequented Param Krupalu Dev's office daily, drawn by His unparalleled spiritual depth. Gandhiji later declared that Param Krupalu Dev was the foremost of his three guides — the other two being Ruskin and Tolstoy. Param Krupalu Dev's influence was so profound that it averted Gandhiji from converting to Christianity or Islam, decisively anchoring him in the spiritual traditions of India.

› First meeting with Param Krupalu Dev

In Jeth of V.S. 1947, Gandhiji — freshly returned from England as a barrister — was introduced to Param Krupalu Dev at the home of Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta in Mumbai. Dr. Pranjivandas was both Param Krupalu Dev's wife's uncle and a close associate of Gandhiji. This introduction set in motion one of the most consequential spiritual relationships of modern India.

Gandhiji was immediately struck by Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary qualities. During his two years practising law in Mumbai, he visited Param Krupalu Dev's office daily. Their conversations ranged across the deepest questions of philosophy, religion, and the nature of the soul.

› Daily association in Mumbai

For approximately two years while practising law in Mumbai, Gandhiji frequented Param Krupalu Dev's office daily. He observed Param Krupalu Dev managing His jewellery business at Revashankar Jagjivan and Co., yet simultaneously remaining absorbed in spiritual contemplation. Gandhiji was deeply impressed by how Param Krupalu Dev conducted worldly affairs without the slightest inner disturbance — a living demonstration that spiritual elevation does not require renunciation of the world.

› Three guides

Gandhiji identified three people who profoundly shaped his life: Shrimad Rajchandraji, Ruskin, and Tolstoy. Of these, he placed Param Krupalu Dev foremost — as his spiritual guide. While Ruskin and Tolstoy influenced his social and political philosophy, it was Param Krupalu Dev who shaped the very foundation of his spiritual life.

› Correspondence from Africa

In the summer of V.S. 1949, Gandhiji left for South Africa, hired as solicitor by Abdullah Sheth, a Muslim businessman from Porbandar. Even from Africa, their correspondence continued until the end of Param Krupalu Dev's life.

From Africa, Gandhiji sent Param Krupalu Dev a letter containing 27 searching questions about the soul, karma, moksha, and related topics. Param Krupalu Dev answered each question with lucid logic and recommended that Gandhiji study Panchikaran, Maniratnamala, Yogvasistha, Shaddarshansamucchay, and Mokshmala. This correspondence proved decisive — it averted Gandhiji from converting to Christianity or Islam at a time when he was seriously considering both.

Only 3 letters from Param Krupalu Dev to Gandhiji are available today. Gandhiji confirmed there were many more, but a bundle of letters was accidentally left behind in a bus in London and never recovered.

› Param Krupalu Dev's influence on Gandhiji

In V.S. 1962, while at Phoenix Ashram near Durban (which he had established in V.S. 1960), Gandhiji took his vow of celibacy — a decision he attributed to Param Krupalu Dev's influence. He used to regularly read Param Krupalu Dev's composition 'Nirkhine Navyauvana'.

Gandhiji returned permanently to India in V.S. 1972. He celebrated Shrimad Rajchandra Jayanti in Rajkot and Wadhwan, and annually observed Kartik Purnima at Kochrab and later Sabarmati Ashram in honour of Param Krupalu Dev.

› After Param Krupalu Dev's passing

On his first visit to Rajkot after Param Krupalu Dev's passing, Gandhiji went straight from the railway station to Param Krupalu Dev's memorial — such was the depth of his reverence.

At the request of Shri Revashankarbhai Jagjivan, who served as chief executive of the Paramshrut Prabhavak Mandal, Gandhiji wrote the preface for the 2nd edition of the book 'Shrimad Rajchandra' (published V.S. 1982).

Gandhiji was assassinated on Posh Vad 5, V.S. 2004 (January 30, 1948) by Nathuram Godse.

› Writings about Param Krupalu Dev

Gandhiji wrote extensively about Param Krupalu Dev throughout his life:

  • Preface for the 2nd edition of 'Shrimad Rajchandra'
  • Chapter titled 'Raychandbhai' in his autobiography
  • Memoirs started while at Yerwada Jail
  • Article 'Some recollections of Raychandbhai'

In the June 1930 issue of the Modern Review, Gandhiji wrote at length about Param Krupalu Dev's extraordinary qualities and impact on his own life.
