## Quotes BY Shrimad Rajchandra

### From Letters (Patrank)

> 'Whether I possess a body or not, is something that I barely know upon recollection.'  
> — Patrank-255

> 'I am supremely detached from this world; even if it were to turn into gold, for me it has no more value than a blade of grass; and being in His Grace, is my place of worship.'  
> — Patrank-214

> 'Renouncing the world externally is not as great as renouncing the world while being internally engrossed in spiritual pursuit.'  
> — Shrimad (on householder path vs. monkhood)

### On His Own Spiritual State

> 'Many are the times I feel the desire to renounce everything and live like a recluse in the forest. But then, I reason with myself – the true forest is within. Renouncing the world externally is not as great as renouncing the world while being internally engrossed in spiritual pursuit.'  
> — Shrimad (recounted in the book)

> He told Shri Kalyanjibhai that He could recollect **800 previous births**.

> He told Shri Khimjibhai Devji: **'You have greatly obliged me'** (referring to an obligation from a past life).

### On Avdhan Powers

> Shrimad considered these powers as **'tinsel'** compared to the **'gold'** of self-realisation.  
> — (paraphrased in the book; Shrimad viewed avdhan as worldly feats unworthy of a soul seeking liberation)

### On Food / Senses

> He mentioned to Pundit Lalan that **He ate through His nose**. (Referring to His extraordinarily keen sense of smell – He could perceive food by scent alone.)

### On the Salt Test

When Chhotalal Revashankar Anjaria secretly added salt to sweet food as a test, Shrimad detected it immediately and said:  
> **'Brother, this test was unnecessary.'**

### On Photographs at Wadhwan Camp

Shrimad later told Mansukhbhai Devshi about the photographs:  
> His state during the photograph session was akin to **Shri Parshwanath during Kamath's torment** – absolute equanimity amidst external provocation.

### On the Open Chest of Jewels (to Nanchandbhai)

When Nanchandbhai expressed concern about the chest of valuable jewels left open, Shrimad replied (summarised):  
> The real danger is not from thieves outside, but from the thief of attachment within. The unguarded valuables are less dangerous than the unguarded mind.

### Final Words & Last Day (from Mansukhbhai's letter)

On Chaitra Vad 5, V.S. 1957 (April 9, 1901), at 2:00 PM:  

> **'Do not worry. Your brother's death will be in the state of samadhi.'** (at 2:30 AM)

> **'Mansukh, do not be sad. Take care of mother. I am now abiding in the Self.'** (at 8:45 AM)

On the day before (Chaitra Vad 4), He told devotees: **"Do not worry, this Soul is eternal. It will certainly attain the highest state. Maintain peace and equanimity."** On Chaitra Vad 5, at 2:30 AM He caught severe cold and said: **"Do not worry. Your brother's death will be in the state of samadhi."** At 8:45 AM He told Mansukhbhai: **"Mansukh, do not be sad. Take care of mother. I am now abiding in the Self."** From 7:45 AM to 2:00 PM He remained in absolute samadhi; His face glowed ever more radiantly as life force receded. At 2:00 PM, the Pure Soul severed its ties with the body.

## Quotes ABOUT Shrimad Rajchandra

### By Mahatma Gandhi

> 'I had come in closest personal touch with him. His judgement appealed to my moral sense in the vast majority of cases. The bedrock of his faith was unquestionably ahimsa. His ahimsa, if it included the tiniest insect, also covered the whole of humanity.'  
> — Gandhiji [Ref. 56 in book]

> 'Raychandbhai's commercial transactions covered hundreds of thousands. He was a connoisseur of pearls and diamonds. No knotty business problem was too difficult for him. But all these things were not the centre round which his life revolved. That centre was the passion to see God face to face. Amongst the things on his business table there were invariably to be found some religious book and his diary. The moment he finished his business he opened the religious book or the diary. Much of his published writings is a reproduction from this diary. The man who, immediately on finishing his talk about weighty business transaction, began to write about the hidden things of the spirit could evidently not be a businessman at all, but a real seeker after Truth. And I saw him thus absorbed in godly pursuits in the midst of business, not once or twice, but very often. I never saw him lose his state of equipoise.'  
> — Gandhiji [Ref. 57 in book]

> 'The result was that I became peaceful. My mind was convinced that whatever I was seeking could be found in Hinduism. Raychandbhai is responsible for bringing about this change, so the reader can have some inkling of the great extent to which my respect for Him must have increased.'  
> — Gandhiji [Ref. 58 in book] (on Shrimad's answers to his 27 questions)

> 'But it was in South Africa that I came to realize the importance of observing brahmacharya even with respect to my wife. I cannot definitely say what circumstance or what book it was, that set my thoughts in that direction, but I have a recollection that the predominant factor was the influence of Raychandbhai.'  
> — Gandhiji [Ref. 60 in book] (on his celibacy vow)

> Gandhiji placed Shrimad at the **forefront** among his three guides (Shrimad, Ruskin, and Tolstoy).

### By Dr. Pranjivandas Mehta (introducing Shrimad to Gandhiji)

> 'He is a poet, yet He is with us in business. He is Enlightened and a shatavdhani.'

### By Munishri Punyavijayji (Jignasu)

> 'Shrimad defeated delusion, while remaining in the house of delusion itself! This can be achieved only by an exceptional, extraordinary, resolute One like Him.'  
> — [Ref. 1 in book]

### By Ramanlal C. Shah (Foreword)

> 'Shrimad Rajchandra is an exceptional self-realised being of our times. A perusal of His spiritual literature, gives us a glimpse of His exalted spiritual state.'

> 'Shrimad's life was like a living philosopher's stone; in fact, even better.'

> 'If Shrimad had, per chance, the longevity of Mahatma Gandhi, there would have been a great revolution in our religious and spiritual fields.'

### By Navalchandbhai (on Shrimad's passing)

> Wrote to Ambalalbhai that Shrimad's face was **extraordinarily radiant** at death – more luminous than ever before.

### By Ambalalbhai (last words, about Shrimad)

> **'Sahajatmaswaroop Hey Prabhu'**  
> — Last words of Ambalalbhai (Chaitra Vad 12, V.S. 1963)

### By Ambalalbhai (devotional verse)

> **'Maha devyaha kukshiratnam...'**  
> — Sanskrit verse composed by Ambalalbhai in praise of Shrimad

## Key Verse from Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra

The opening verse establishing the Six Fundamentals (paraphrased from the book's exposition):

> The soul exists; it is eternal; it is the doer of its actions; it is the receiver of consequences; liberation exists; and the path to liberation exists – this is samyaktva (right faith).
